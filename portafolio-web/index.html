PROJECT 01

Complete Street on National Route 2

Transportation | StreetsSmart mobilityPérez Zeledón, Costa Rica
All projectsNext

A 1.5 km redesign of the Inter-American Highway through San Isidro de El General, turning a four-lane, car-first corridor into a street for people who walk, cycle, take the bus and drive.

National Route 2 crosses the urban core of Pérez Zeledón with a two-way centre turn lane, dozens of uncontrolled accesses and no room for bicycles. COSEVI crash maps put the canton's worst clusters on this stretch. Using municipal counts (MDPZ, 2021), aerial imagery and field visits, I mapped every vehicle conflict point and designed a complete-street alternative.

The design removes all left turns along the corridor and concentrates turning movements in two roundabouts. Lanes narrow to 3.25 m, enough for the articulated design truck with 0.65 m between vehicles, and below the 3.65 m the SIECA manual prefers, as a deliberate traffic-calming measure. Each side gets a 2.0 m protected bike lane behind a 10 cm curb, a 2.5 m sidewalk and a planted strip of shade trees in a valley where temperatures run from 19 to 30 °C. Crossings follow the desire lines seen on site, and 2,120 m of open roadside ditch become buried storm pipe.

I modelled the corridor in Civil 3D, ran SUMO micro-simulations for today's traffic and for 2035, and priced two scenarios, with and without two 140 m grade separations, including a social payback based on crash costs. The simulations also showed the limit of the design: without better transit and a heavy-vehicle bypass, the travel-time gains fade within ten years.

PROJECT 02

K206 Roundabout Detour

Transportation | RoadsIntersectionsPedestrians & transitArea 03, Bildøy · Rv. 555 Sotrasambandet
PreviousNext

A temporary roundabout and three connecting roads that cleared the site of the K206 grade separation while cars, buses and people on foot kept moving.

For Phase 2, construction needed the whole area around the K206 grade separation free. Rv. 555 allows no left-turning junctions along its length, so the detour joins the local roads through a roundabout. I set the roundabout and its three approaches (alignments A, B and C) to follow the levels and alignment of the permanent roads they meet wherever the geometry allowed, so that the material placed for the detour serves the final works and the volume of fill stays low.

The detour also had to work for public transport and pedestrians: a U-turn for buses at the end of one arm, a bus stop on another, sidewalks on the streets that needed them and safe crossing points laid out following Statens vegvesen handbook V127, Kryssingssteder for gående (crossing points for pedestrians).

It was one of the most demanding detours on the project. We developed and compared several layouts with Construction, Traffic and Temporary Works before finding one that served pedestrians, buses and private cars at the same time. PROJECT 03

Storavatnet Temporary Roundabout

Transportation | RoadsIntersectionsConstruction phasingArea 11, Storavatnet · Rv. 555 Sotrasambandet
PreviousNext

A two-phase temporary roundabout at one of the busiest junctions in Norway, designed so that each phase change and each fix to the existing road could be done in a few nights.

At Storavatnet the project has to build two new bridges parallel to the existing main road, demolish and rebuild an underpass beneath it and put up a new retaining wall. That closes the link that ran under the road, so traffic from the north and from the west needed a new connection. I designed a temporary roundabout to carry all of it, with five new alignments: A, B, C1, C2 and Ramp AB.

The layout had to avoid rock blasting east of the roundabout, tie into the existing ramp between the two roundabouts to the north, keep the north–east ramp open, follow existing road levels so the temporary road is quick to build, separate opposing lanes physically, and give the bypass curves suitable speeds without clashing with the excavation for the new underpass.

Two phases, one night to switch. The retaining wall is built in five sections over two phases, so the detour has two phases as well. Phase 1 can stay at the level of the existing road. Phase 2 has to pass over the finished part of the wall, clear the Phase 2 excavation and the underpass under construction, and still meet Phase 1 exactly, so the switch can be made in a single night. Getting there meant using the lowest superelevation the standard allows on the curves while staying safe, and a lot of work on the levels.

A curve that leaned the wrong way. The ramp and the east approach turn right over a carriageway with a −5 % crossfall: adverse superelevation for those curves, and not acceptable with winter coming. The Traffic department asked for it to be corrected under a short night diversion. I tightened the transition within the Norwegian standard until the new surface met the old one cleanly. Most of the tie-in changes by 4 cm or less and can be paved in one asphalt layer while it stays open; the deeper excavation is kept clear of the ramps, so the correction fits in three nights.

PROJECT 04

Kiplevatnet Grouting Platform

Site grading & earthworksDrainageTemporary worksArea 09, Drotningsvik · Rv. 555 Sotrasambandet
PreviousNext

A work platform for grouting the rock above a shallow tunnel, fitted between a road that had to stay open, buried storm-water pipes and a lake.

A tunnel under construction passed close to the surface at Kiplevatnet, so the rock mass above it had to be strengthened by grouting before the tunnel face reached that point. Construction needed a level platform for the drilling and grouting rigs and a separate plant area for the workshop, water tank, mixer, pump and silo slab.

The constraints set the geometry. The adjacent road had to stay open at its existing level for the whole operation, so the platform ties into it without changing its grade. Two lagoons, 294 m² and 136 m² with their bottom at +41.88, give at least 400 m³ of retention. Two Ø500 mm storm-water pipes run directly underneath, so every level keeps at least 80 cm of cover over them, with a Ø1500 mm, 3.5 m deep manhole on the line next to the plant area.

I graded the platform in Civil 3D against the existing terrain and the bedrock surface, keeping the earthworks small and entirely in soil: 714 m³ of excavation, 601 m³ of fill and no rock excavation. The plan, sections and IFC models of the platform, pipes and manhole were issued in March 2024.

PROJECT 05

Rebuilding Roads That Stay in Service

Transportation | RoadsPavement & earthworksClient coordinationArea 03, Bildøy
PreviousNext

A technical clarification to the Norwegian Public Roads Administration on how to rebuild three roads that remain open and carry permanent utilities.

In Area 03 the new alignments of roads 203000, 203100 and 203400 partly overlap roads already in use, some with permanent utilities underneath. Full excavation would have meant relocating services and disturbing pavements that have long since settled. I redesigned the roads in Novapoint and prepared Technical Clarification TA-254, proposing to keep structurally sound existing pavement, absorb level differences inside the new layer package, build the longitudinal and transverse transitions to the project's Design Basis (plan F003) and re-lay the full wearing course.

Where deep blasting is required, a drain pipe at the lowest point of the excavation keeps the lower layers dry, following Håndbok N200 §1.3.4.2. NPRA asked for an overview of where existing structure would be kept and where the road is widened or re-profiled, which became Revision 1 of the TA in June 2026. confirmar tu rol exacto en el TA y la respuesta final de NPRA

The same 3D-model discipline goes into reviewing other people's work. For a design claim in 2025 I checked the designer's road models at nine critical chainages on eight roads and documented missing soil and deep-blasting layers with cross-section evidence.

PROJECT 06

GIS & BIM for 245 Road Models

Infrastructure & planningGISBIM coordinationRv. 555, 11 construction areas
PreviousProfile

Turning hundreds of separate road models into maps and coordination models that owners, planners and site teams can use.

The road network on Sotrasambandet is delivered as roughly 250 road models across eleven construction areas, from the main highway to footpaths, roundabouts and private accesses. The future owners, from the state and Vestland County to the municipalities and private landowners, need to know what they will receive, and site teams need to know what gets built in each phase.

I built a GIS layer of every road as polygons, classified by road type, owner and construction phase (1A, 1B, 2A, 2B), converting CAD hatches into GIS polygons with my own AutoLISP routine and publishing them as shapefiles and a QGIS project. For Vestland County I assembled Navisworks coordination models per area, combining the road models with terrain surfaces clipped to the project limits. A Dynamo script extracts start and end chainages for all alignments, about 57 km of road axes, for planning and quantities.
